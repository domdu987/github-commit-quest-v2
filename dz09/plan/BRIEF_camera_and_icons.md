# Director's brief: camera-angle pass and themed icon effects (requested by the user)

The user asked for two things on top of the shot plan:
1. More camera angles, chosen with the skill's camera grammar (`spec/camera_motion.md`, `references/look.md`, `v3/stage`).
2. Graphic icon effects in the film's theme that communicate things to the viewer (the character sheet already uses this language: yellow "!" spark lines over James, the swirl over Phum, the "!" ticks over Ton).

## Camera rules for this pass
- Every shot names its angle: low, eye, high, top-down (overhead), Dutch (tilted 8 to 15 degrees), over-the-shoulder, POV, insert/macro. Lens 22 mm default; 35 to 50 mm only for phone and face inserts.
- No two consecutive shots share the same angle and size, unless the repeat is a deliberate rhythm beat.
- Angle carries meaning: low on Kong when he acts (strength); high on Kong when the phone call makes him small; Dutch only once the horror begins (from the driver lifting his head onwards); top-down for the gridlock scale and for the camera-rises-above-the-expressway ending; POV through the cracked windscreen or from inside the car for the driver reveal.
- Handheld energy only where emotion spikes (state amplitude in degrees/cm), locked-off for the phone call so the shake later feels earned.
- At least 5 different angle types inside the 55 s, and a whip or snap transition no more than twice.
- Two framings: every shot must state a 9:16 framing and a 16:9 framing (same 3D scene, second camera or wider sensor). Note where 16:9 needs extra set width or a different subject placement.

## Icon effect library (flat 2D, same ink style as the puppets: black outline, flat fill, slightly wobbly hand-drawn line)
Each icon pops in with a 4-frame scale overshoot (0 -> 115% -> 100%), holds, and pops out; it gets a short sound accent from the SFX list. Icons are emission-only cards in the 3D scene (never lit), or screen-space overlays when they explain a rule. Use them to communicate, not to decorate: about one every 4 to 6 seconds, never two at once except in the climax.

The 16:9 version must carry MORE detail than the 9:16 short, not a wider crop of it: the wider frame shows more of the world (more cars, people, signs, skyline, side lanes, extra background action and props), richer set dressing at frame left and right, and recomposed shots. 9:16 is the teaser that sends viewers to the 16:9 on YouTube.
