# 04 — Per-clip story formula for AI-animated vertical story clips (craft layer), as of 2026-10-31

Scope: the structure of the clip itself (hook, story shape, retention devices, ending, follow conversion, text/voice/sound, pacing, series design, cadence vs quality) for 30–180 s vertical AI-animated story content on Instagram Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts. Ranking signals/policy and Thai audience taste are covered by other researchers; they are referenced here only where they constrain craft. Primary case study (per coordinator addendum): Prompt KUB (IG @promptkub), whose formula derives from @stickocalypse_shorts.

Evidence-quality legend used throughout: **[platform]** = platform-published data/guidance; **[verified]** = I retrieved the number myself on 2026-10-31 (yt-dlp / page metadata / ygnos.com data file); **[owner]** = owner-claimed (ygnos.com course/brand pages or coordinator relay); **[vendor]** = analytics/tool vendor blog with no published methodology; **[press]** = journalism; **[folk]** = widely repeated creator advice with no data found.

## KQ1. Hooks: what the first 1–3 seconds should do, and which hook templates have measured results

### Takeaway
The only platform-published numbers are from TikTok's ad research (90% of recall impact and 80% of awareness impact land within 6 s; suspense in the opening +16% watch time; showing a person in the first 2 s +50% "hooking power"; the word "you" within 5 s +128% purchase intent); YouTube's official Shorts metric is "Stayed to watch" past "the initial seconds". The "3-second rule" numbers circulating in creator blogs (33–65% scroll past in 3 s; 0.9–1.5 s decision time) are vendor claims without methodology. For animated stories, the evidence-consistent hook is a cold-open image of a concrete situation plus a second-person conditional spoken line and a 3–6-word on-screen title in the first 2 s.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok Creative Codes (TikTok's own creative guidance): "The Hook: The first six seconds are vital"; "90% of ad recall impact is captured within 6 seconds" and "80% of the awareness impact is captured within 6 seconds" (source: TikTok "Value of View" analysis, 2020); "Suspense: ads that use suspense early in a story lead to a +16% increase in watch time"; "Surprise … 1.7x VTR"; "Emotion … 1.7x lift in awareness" (source: TikTok Marketing Science US SMB Creative Effectiveness Study 2021, Lumen); suggested narrative structures: "a-ha moments, problem-solutions, or a customer journey"; structure = hook / body / close with "a strong CTA" — [TikTok Creative Codes PDF](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_ENG.pdf) **[platform; ad context]**
- TikTok × CreatorIQ attention report (Dec 2023): showing a creator or any person in the first couple of seconds raises "hooking power" by 50% and ad recognition by 32%; the creator saying "you" within the first five seconds is linked to a 128% boost in purchase intent; a direct greeting to viewers to a 112% uplift in brand recall; "most recall and awareness impact occurs in the first six seconds" — [Marketing Brew](https://marketingbrew.com/stories/2023/12/18/pay-attention-to-me-report-shares-brand-tips-for-catching-users-interest-on-tiktok) **[platform-sourced via press; ad context]**

- Stickocalypse hook template (second-person conditional + anti-obvious-move + "survive except for one mistake") — [Vuela blog](https://vuela.ai/blog/how-to-make-viral-stick-figure-series-with-ai) **[vendor]**; Prompt KUB's hook spec "ฮุก 2 วินาทีแรก" — [ygnos.com/workshop](https://ygnos.com/workshop) **[owner]**; AI Bible trend hook is a caption-only premise "If Daniel had an iPhone…" — [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/ai-bible-influencer-videos-ai-bible-stories) **[press]**

### Inferences
- For AI animation (no human face to show in the first 2 s), the closest substitute for TikTok's "person in frame +50%" finding is a character with a readable face/pose in a recognisable, local situation (Prompt KUB's farmer-and-dog kitchen; school chaa-yen cart), plus second-person narration ("ถ้าคุณ…") which maps to the "you within 5 s" finding.
- Hook templates that recur across every winning case here: (1) conditional premise "ถ้า… แล้วคุณอยู่ที่…" (Stickocalypse/Prompt KUB), (2) incongruity premise "If X had an iPhone" (AI Bible), (3) escalating-misfortune open on a crying character + melancholic music (AI cats), (4) in-medias-res confrontation (micro-dramas). Templates 1 and 4 fit survival/story; 2 fits comedy.
- Treat "first 3 seconds" as the swipe window and "first 6–15 seconds" as the commitment window: TikTok's data points cluster at 6 s; YouTube's named example uses 15 s.

### Gaps
- No platform publishes creator-side (non-ad) hook-retention data; I found no A/B data for visual vs spoken vs text hooks specifically for animation.

## KQ2. Story structure for 30 s, 60 s, 90 s and 2–3 min; beats/shots per 10 s; midpoint re-hook

### Takeaway
Every successful short story format found here is causal-chain storytelling (South Park's "but/therefore") compressed to one problem → one surprising fact → one new problem, with a twist and a forward hook; winning animated clips cut every 2–3 s (≈4 shots/10 s) and change narrative beat every 3–5 s. Length evidence favours 20–60 s for completion and 60–90 s for total watch time; nothing in the evidence supports going past ~90 s for a serialized AI story unless it carries two reversals.

### Cited Findings

- The midpoint re-hook should sit at or just before the median exit point (≈33 s on Prompt KUB), so that the half-watchers still receive a share-worthy fact (see KQ9).

### Inferences
- The HUD card is a "payoff-promise" device: a countdown or gauge visibly ticking (30 min to grid failure; 57 cm water level) converts the abstract open loop into a concrete, glanceable one, and doubles as a pattern interrupt without needing a new scene. ## KQ4. Endings and loops: seamless loops vs cliffhangers; Part 1/Part 2 structures

### Takeaway
Cliffhanger endings stated as a concrete threat plus a numbered next episode are the convention of every serialized winner here (Stickocalypse, Prompt KUB, micro-dramas, Fruit Love Island); seamless loops are a rewatch tactic for non-narrative clips and are structurally incompatible with a cliffhanger. Numbered "Part N" sequels reliably lose 40–94% of the premiere's views, so parts work for follower conversion and episode-to-episode watch, not for reach.

### Cited Findings
- Stickocalypse: every part ends on a cliffhanger "with a concrete threat, not a question to the viewer" ("Someone was already inside the fence."); parts are numbered and continue — [Vuela](https://vuela.ai/blog/how-to-make-viral-stick-figure-series-with-ai) **[vendor]**; measured decay Part 1 6.04M → Part 2 380K → Part 4 242K → Part 5 131K → Part 6 79K → Part 8 15.8K — YouTube metadata **[verified]**

### Inferences
- Rule: a clip ends either in a cliffhanger (serial) or in a loop (standalone), never both. For Prompt KUB's serial format the ending is: twist → one-sentence concrete threat → "ตอนต่อไป" card (hold 1–2 s), no outro, no spoken CTA.

- YouTube's loop exclusion from engaged views means loop tricks inflate public views, not the metrics YouTube says it uses for engagement/earnings; on IG/TikTok the loop-completion claim is vendor folklore.

### Gaps
- No platform data on whether "Part 1/Part 2" structures raise or lower follower growth; only the view-decay pattern is observable.

## KQ6. Voice, text and sound for AI animation: TTS vs real voice, consistency, subtitles, music licensing, lip-sync, avoiding the "AI slop" look

### Inferences
- For a Thai narrator, the only ElevenLabs options are v3/v4; v4 is the documented choice for long-form consistency. Keep one voice ID, fixed stability/similarity settings, and write emphasis/pauses into the script (short sentences, one-word beats) to defeat the "TTS plateau".
- Lip-sync threshold: the winning stick/vector formats avoid lip-sync entirely (narration over characters who don't speak on camera). If characters must speak, the failure modes that get called out are b/p/m closures and vowel sameness; cutting away from the mouth on dialogue lines is the cheapest fix.
- Subtitle style that both case channels converge on: large, centred, high-contrast burned-in text, 2–6 words per card, timed to the narration (Prompt KUB: per-character timing). Burn-in is also what makes clips platform-portable (IG/TikTok/YT) without re-editing.
- Repetition risk is real on YouTube specifically: a channel that posts the same template 3×/day with the same voice should vary settings, HUD types and openings per episode and keep the narration non-identical, or it meets the "repeated narration/templated Shorts" description.

## KQ9 (addendum). Why "new beat every 3–5 s + HUD + survival stakes + ตอนต่อไป" yields a 33-s median watch time on a 60–70-s clip and high follow conversion — and its measurable weaknesses

### Takeaway
The structure stacks the three devices that platform data rewards (early suspense, dense scene/text change, sound-on narration) with a serial promise, which plausibly produces ~50% average retention and ~2.7 follows per 1,000 views; its weaknesses are that most viewers do not reach the end (completion is probably 25–40%), sequels lose half their audience each episode, the template is exposed to repetition/originality rules, and the cadence invites fatigue and backlash.

### Cited Findings
- Owner metrics: median AWT 33.0 s on 60–70-s clips; median reach 437,069; like rates 6–9% on story clips — [ygnos.com/brand](https://ygnos.com/brand); [ygnos.com/data/ig-timeline.json](https://ygnos.com/data/ig-timeline.json) **[owner]**
- Mechanism evidence: suspense early → +16% watch time; fast scene changes and text pop-ups sustain attention; sound-on 73% stop-and-look — [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_ENG.pdf) **[platform]**; micro-decision viewing and absent stopping points — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/microdramas-are-booming-ai-could-make-it-even-harder-to-stop-binge-watching-291962) **[academic op-ed]**; "ตอนต่อไป"/cliffhanger convention — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microdrama); [Rocket Media Lab](https://rocketmedialab.co/short-drama-7apps-insights/)
- Length/completion trade-off (vendor): >60-s videos average 48% completion vs 62% for 21–34 s; retention below 45% "problematic" — [Opus.pro](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-length-format-retention-data) **[vendor]**
- Reported ranking inputs on Instagram include average watch time plus "relative watch time (the share of the video watched) and absolute watch time", likes per reach and sends per reach (attributed to Mosseri, secondary) — [Socialinsider](https://socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm/); [Fanpage Karma](https://www.fanpagekarma.com/insights/?p=9254) **[secondary]**
- Repetition/originality constraints: YouTube's inauthentic-content examples ("nearly identical narrated stories", "templated Shorts") — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660); Instagram requires reposts to be "significantly changed" (voiceover, unique text, creative edits qualify) — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator) **[press on policy]**
- Creative fatigue: TikTok's own guidance says to "combat creative fatigue by refreshing your content often with different assets" — [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_ENG.pdf) **[platform]**
- Series decay and burnout cases: Stickocalypse Part 1→2 −94%; Prompt KUB EP1→EP2 −59%; Fruit Love Island ended after 15 days amid removals and backlash; the Thai AI monk handle is gone — see KQ0, KQ8.2, KQ8 **[verified/press]**
- Audience criticisms of AI story content: "clichéd", "cold and mechanical", "made to showcase AI" — [Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1015691) **[press]**

### Inferences
- Why 33 s: a 65-s clip with median AWT 33 s = ~51% average retention. With a front-loaded curve (swipe-away in the first 2–3 s, then slow decay), that average is consistent with roughly 70–80% staying past 3 s, ~50% present at the midpoint twist, and ~25–40% reaching "ตอนต่อไป". The structure keeps the decay slow because every 3–5 s a new fact/number/HUD change gives the viewer a reason to re-commit (the micro-decision mechanism), and the survival premise supplies continuous suspense (the +16% device).
- Why follows: the follow is earned at the end card by the people who are most invested (the ~25–40% who reach it) plus by those who return for EP2 through the series name; 2.7 follows per 1,000 views is consistent with ≈1% of end-reachers following.
- Weakness 1 — completion: if only ~a third finish, the twist/cliffhanger (the share and follow trigger) is unseen by most viewers; moving a share-worthy fact to ~25–30 s (just before the median exit) would let half-watchers carry something to share.
- Weakness 2 — no rewatch loop: a cliffhanger ending prevents the seamless-loop rewatch that boosts public view counts on IG/TikTok; the format trades loops for follows.
- Weakness 3 — episode decay: 50–60% loss per episode means a 3-episode arc yields ~1.4–1.7× the premiere's views in total; more, shorter arcs (EP1 every 1–2 days) beat long serials for reach.
- Weakness 4 — template exposure: 3+/day of the same narrator, structure and HUD language fits YouTube's "templated Shorts/repeated narration" description on that platform specifically; on IG the adaptation is defensible as "significantly changed" but each adapted episode should keep the visible credit and original visuals.
- Weakness 5 — fatigue/backlash: the evidence on Fruit Love Island and the Chinese AI dramas shows audiences turn on AI series that feel mass-produced; rotating settings (farm, condo, mountain, school) and genres (survival, business parable) is the channel's existing mitigation.
- Weakness 6 — YouTube-specific view accounting: views now count at start of play (Aug 24 2026) and engaged views exclude loops, so a high-AWT clip is favoured on YouTube only if it also clears the "stayed to watch" gate in the first seconds.

### Gaps
- No retention curve for any Prompt KUB clip is public; the completion estimate is inferred. No platform states how AWT is weighted against completion for Reels.

## KQ10 (addendum). Generalised per-clip template (second-by-second) for a Thai AI-animation story channel — 30 s / 60 s / 90 s variants

### Takeaway
A single spine, scaled by the number of problem→fact→fix loops: open on a concrete Thai situation with a second-person conditional and one on-screen number; run one causal loop per ~12–15 s with a cut every 2–3 s; place a share-worthy fact just before the midpoint; twist at ~85% of runtime; end on a concrete threat + "ตอนต่อไป" card, no outro, no spoken CTA.

### Cited Findings (the template is derived from these; see earlier sections for the full citations)
- Hook: second-person conditional, anti-obvious move, "one mistake" promise; concrete numbers; a cut every 2–3 s; one-word lines; concrete-threat cliffhanger; numbered parts — [Vuela on Stickocalypse](https://vuela.ai/blog/how-to-make-viral-stick-figure-series-with-ai) **[vendor]**
- Engine: "ปัญหา → ข้อเท็จจริง → ทางแก้ ทุก 3–5 วินาที", HUD card, one animal, twist, "ตอนต่อไป", "ฮุก 2 วินาทีแรก", 60–70 s — [ygnos.com/brand](https://ygnos.com/brand); [ygnos.com/workshop](https://ygnos.com/workshop) **[owner]**
- Timing data: 90% of recall impact within 6 s; suspense early +16% watch time; text pop-ups and scene changes sustain attention; strong CTA at the close — [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_ENG.pdf) **[platform]**; conflict within 15 s; 60-s scenes — [YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/the-evolution-of-web-series-on-youtube-microdramas/) **[platform]**; tension point every ~30 s; end on a question — [Rocket Media Lab](https://rocketmedialab.co/short-drama-7apps-insights/) **[press]**; mid-video re-hooks ~15 s/30 s, final-frame hold 1–2 s, no outro — [Opus.pro](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-length-format-retention-data); [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/perfect-youtube-short-length-structure-hooks) **[vendor]**
- Causal chaining: "but/therefore", never "and then" — [Matt Rickard](https://blog.matt-rickard.com/p/but-therefore) **[craft]**
- Sound/voice: sound-on first; calm consistent narrator (ElevenLabs v3/v4 for Thai); burned-in large subtitles — [TikTok/Kantar](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/id/blog/kantar-report-how-brands-are-making-noise-and-driving-impact-with-sound-on-tiktok); [ElevenLabs models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models); [ygnos.com](https://ygnos.com)
- Caption: hook line first, ≤200 characters, 3–5 topic + 1 brand + 1 format hashtag; credit line when adapted — [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/reports/tiktok-strategy-insights); reel captions [Den1l8AO-a1](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Den1l8AO-a1/) **[verified]**

### Inferences — the template
**60–70 s master (target ≈22–26 shots, 14–18 beats, 110–150 Thai words of narration)**
- 0.0–2.0 s | SHOT 1 (cold open, calm ordinary scene with the animal visible) | NARRATION: "ถ้า[ภัย]มาตอนที่คุณอยู่[สถานที่ไทยที่คุ้นเคย]…" | TEXT: title card 3–6 words (same as caption line 1) | HUD: one element appears (clock/number/map pin) | SOUND: ambient + first music hit on the cut.
- 2–5 s | SHOT 2 | the obvious move and why it fails ("ส่วนใหญ่จะ[วิ่งเข้าเมือง] — ผิด") | HUD: the first number (time limit / distance / headcount).
- 5–8 s | SHOT 3 | the promise: "คุณรอดได้… ถ้าไม่พลาดอย่างเดียว" (open loop #1 that pays off only in the next episode).
- 8–20 s | SHOTS 4–8 | LOOP 1 = ปัญหา → ข้อเท็จจริง → ทางแก้ (e.g., water first; the pump is electric; fill every container before the grid drops) | HUD updates each beat (countdown ticks, inventory count) | one-word line as a beat ("อย่า.").
- 15 s | re-hook: a new threat enters the frame or the countdown jumps (the "conflict within 15 s" point).
- 20–30 s | SHOTS 9–12 | LOOP 2 with a cost ("เครื่องปั่นไฟ = เสียง = ดึงมันมา") | the animal reacts (emotion beat) | share-worthy fact lands at ~27–30 s so half-watchers leave with something to tell.
- 30–33 s | SHOT 13 | MIDPOINT REVERSAL: a fact that flips the plan (setting change #2) | music drops to silence for 1 s, then resumes.
- 33–48 s | SHOTS 14–19 | LOOP 3: new problem created by the fix; a newcomer/alarm object; numbers again | HUD: a second element (map/route) | text cards every 3–5 s.
- 48–55 s | SHOTS 20–22 | consequence: the "one mistake" is set up but not shown; stakes stated with a number.
- 55–60 s | SHOT 23 | TWIST: the mistake/threat revealed in one sentence.
- 60–65 s | SHOT 24 | CLIFFHANGER as concrete threat ("มีคนอยู่ในรั้วแล้ว") → "ตอนต่อไป" card with series name + EP number (hold 1.5–2 s) | no outro, no spoken "follow"; the only CTA is text on the card ("ตอนต่อไป" / "EP.2 พรุ่งนี้").
- Caption: line 1 = hook; line 2 = series/EP; 3–5 topic hashtags + #PromptKUB + #AIanimation; credit if adapted. Pinned comment: the question the next episode answers + link to the previous EP.

**30 s variant (≈9–12 shots, 5–7 beats, ~60–75 words)**
- 0–2 s hook (conditional + number on HUD) → 2–6 s obvious move fails → 6–16 s one loop (problem → fact → fix) → 16–22 s cost/new problem → 22–27 s twist → 27–30 s cliffhanger + "ตอนต่อไป". Use for premieres of a new world (EP1), for 15-s brand cut-downs, and for TikTok where sub-35-s completion is highest.

**90 s variant (≈32–38 shots, 22–26 beats, ~170–210 words)**
- 0–8 s hook block as above → 8–30 s LOOP 1 + re-hook at 15 s → 30–33 s reversal #1 → 33–55 s LOOP 2 with the animal/newcomer and a second HUD element → 55–58 s reversal #2 (re-hook) → 58–78 s LOOP 3 (cost) → 78–85 s twist → 85–90 s cliffhanger + card. Only use when the script genuinely has two reversals; otherwise the extra 30 s lowers completion (Stickocalypse's 87-s and 168-s parts under-performed its 61-s premiere).

**Series design rules derived from the decay data**
- Every episode must also work as someone's EP1: restate the premise in the first 2 s and in the title (not just "Ep. 2").
- Prefer 2–3-episode arcs launched often over long serials; launch a new world/premise at least every 2–3 days, since EP1s are where reach and follows come from.
- Vary setting, HUD type, threat and genre across arcs (survival, business parable, urban hazard) to defeat template fatigue and the "repeated narration" description on YouTube.
- Add one participatory arc per week (viewers vote on the character's next decision in comments; read 1–2 comments on the end card next episode) — the only mechanism with seven-figure follower evidence.

### Gaps
- The second-by-second timings are a synthesis of the Stickocalypse shot cadence, the owner's 3–5-s beat rule and platform timing data; they have not been A/B tested. The writer should present them as a starting template to be tuned against the channel's own retention graphs.

## KQ11. Production cadence vs quality: what fast-growing AI-animation channels publish and how they batch-produce

### Takeaway
Cadence among the fast growers ranges from ~1/week (long-form AI Bible animation) to 2+/day (Fruit Love Island; Prompt KUB ~3–8/day), and the ones sustaining daily output do it with deterministic pipelines (code/SVG rigs, fixed voice, templated HUD) rather than generative video models, which still require hundreds of regenerations for consistency. Quality collapse shows up as audience backlash and removals (Fruit Love Island) or demonetisation risk (YouTube's inauthentic-content rule), not as lower reach per se.

### Inferences
- The quality-at-cadence advantage of the Prompt KUB/Stickocalypse pipeline is structural: rig-based vector animation has zero character drift, so the per-episode cost is script + voice + timing, not regeneration lotteries. Adding generative-video shots (Kling/Veo) should be reserved for 1–2 "hero" shots per episode where drift is tolerable.
- Batch plan consistent with the evidence: write 6–10 episodes per sitting with but/therefore outlines; generate narration in one voice session (consistent settings); auto-time subtitles; render HUD from a shared component library; QA each clip against the 12-tell checklist (hands, text gibberish, metronomic cadence) before scheduling 2–3/day.
- Diminishing returns appear in every dataset (heaviest uploaders earn less; Fruit Love Island burned out at ~2/day); a sustainable ceiling for one creator is probably 2–3 story clips/day with one new premise every 2–3 days.

### Gaps
- No creator publishes per-clip production time for this exact pipeline beyond the vendor's 25-minute replica; no data on the cadence at which Instagram reach per clip starts to fall.
