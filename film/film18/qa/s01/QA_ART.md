## Verdict: FIX-FIRST
The image is beautiful from about g 45 on. But a hook has to read on a phone in the first second, and here it does not: for 0.8 s you see a wall, empty dirt and a dark blob.

## Changes (in priority order)
1. **Start closer (g 0).** Open on a medium-wide framing similar to the current g 45: Tian Dan at about 35-40 % of frame height (head ~y 520-600, feet ~y 1050), with the near dressed ox and its gold dragon filling the middle third. Keep the push at 8 %/s from there so it ends near the current g 77 framing or slightly tighter, and do not push from wide to medium. Lower the camera look-at, or tilt down about 3-4°, so the herd line sits at about y 0.45 and the empty dirt foreground shrinks to under 20 % of the frame.
2. **Remove the dark sack/brush prop on the right (g 0-24).** It is the biggest shape in the opening, it is not a lantern, and it vanishes mid-shot. Replace it with the planned warm lantern 1-2 D from the lens, either bottom-left or bottom-right. Keep it partly in frame for the whole shot and let its glow throw a warm pool on the near ground. That fills the dead lower third and keeps the brief's "foreground lantern".
3. **Make the act read as dressing the ox (whole take, most needed g 0-40).** Have the red dragon coat run from his hands onto the back of the ox he is beside, half on and half hanging, so the pull visibly draws it across the ox. Alternatively, put him on the far side of an undressed ox, flipping the coat over it. Turn the ox's head toward camera with an unimpressed look to add a reaction beat. Keep the stern deadpan face.
4. **Bigger tug snaps, one early.** Put the first punch-in on the first strong tug, within g 6-12, so the strange act lands within 0.4 s. Make the snaps about 1.5x their current size to lift p90 toward 12. Keep the heads above y 880.

Not blocking: the gold dragons, the night palette and the brazier glow read well at phone size once the subject is big, so keep the lighting and costumes as they are.
