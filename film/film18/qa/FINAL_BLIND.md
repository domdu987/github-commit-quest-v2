# Blind viewing: oxclerk_v1.mp4

1. Hook (0-3 s): A night camp with rows of torches, and hundreds of oxen in red dragon-pattern blankets. A small clerk figure in a black hat stands among them and has blades tied to the horns of every ox. The narration "this man caught over a thousand oxen, dressed them in red dragon cloth and tied knives to their horns" is a strong image. The oddity is a clerk in charge of an armed ox army. The hook is stated in the caption and then paid off at 5 s, when the film cuts to a map and a 2000-years-ago explainer. The tight close-up on the clerk at 3-4.5 s is a little stiff.

2. Mockery: The Yan general (gold helmet, big moustache, red armour) laughs at Tian Dan, the "market clerk", and his oxen (20-28 s). He says he will let the oxen chase him home if they can do anything. His soldiers then nickname Tian Dan "the ox clerk" (28-31 s). The mockery is about a former market clerk with livestock facing a trained army. At 36-40 s the general toasts and jeers "the ox clerk surrendered, beef tomorrow!" He is mocking a fake surrender.

3. Mechanism: Tian Dan feigns surrender, so the Yan army relaxes and stops keeping watch. At night he breaks holes in the wall. Over 1000 oxen have oil-soaked reeds tied to their tails (44-47 s) and blades on their horns. The tails are lit and the oxen stampede out through the holes. The fire is behind them, so they can only run forward into the Yan camp. In the dark the Yan soldiers see only dragon-patterned fire monsters with shining blades (53-58 s). The townspeople beat bronze pots to add terror. The larger army routs in one night and Qi retakes over 70 cities. The few win by trickery, panic and a weaponised animal stampede, not by force.

4. Ending: The general swore to go home if the oxen could do anything. At 65-70 s he does go home, as promised, but he is running in terror ahead of the fiery oxen the whole way (the closing frame shows him mid-sprint with the oxen behind him). His own boast is turned into a literal joke: "the oxen chased me home". It is a clean callback.

5. Confusing moments:
- 5-12 s: the map section is fast, with a long list of numbers (70 cities, 2 left, Jimo). The map is a tilted 3D terrain with tiny red flags. The 70+ cities lost and the two cities left are hard to read at phone size.
- 12.5-15 s: Tian Dan appears as a faint ghost overlay on the map and then in a market scene. The older bearded man in the white hat is at first ambiguous with the other characters. At 13-15 s the bearded elder is next to a younger clerk, so it is unclear which one is Tian Dan. At 44-47 s the white-bearded man in a hat is the one tying reeds to the oxen tails, but the hook clerk is the black-hat young man, so this is probably the same person played by two different designs. That looks like a character inconsistency.
- 17-19 s: counts "ten, hundred, thousand oxen" are only partly shown (a blurred shot, then a crowd). The numbers are read, not seen.
- 32-33.5 s: the surrender envoy is small and carries a blank white flag. It reads OK but the Jimo wall sits in a hard-to-follow angle.
- 40.5-43 s: "the wall is pierced with dozens of holes". The holes are visible but small and low in the frame.
- 47.5-50 s: the oxen charge out, but the silhouettes of Yan soldiers appear only at 50 s. The camp is barely shown, so the "charge into the camp" is not visible until 53 s.
- 58-60 s: the pot beating by townspeople on a roof. It is dark and short and does not read clearly as "pots banging".
- 60-65 s: the Yan rout is shown as small figures around a campfire with little visible panic. The words say the army scattered, but the picture shows a calm camp. The "retake 70 cities" is just a map pass.
