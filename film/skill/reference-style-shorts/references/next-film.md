# Design lessons for the next film

Each item below was repaired in 2D at the end of the first full production, or could not be repaired at all. Each one is cheaper to do in 3D, or to decide on the opening, from the start. Read this before phases 4 to 6 (look, opening, shot plan).

## Staging and sets

- **Build depth into the staging, not into a filter.** Sharp picture: no depth-of-field blur (client decision). Depth comes from real 3D parallax, cast shadows, overlap and light: a dark foreground layer near the lens, the lit subject band, a far wall or horizon; two-shots with the near head 1.36 to 1.46 times the far one; a camera that moves so the layers slide. Keep readable things the shot needs beside the subject, large and lit, rather than half hidden behind a head.
- **Keep the caption band free** (y 880 to 950 of 1280): no heads and no text in the picture there. Check it at planning time, not at the conform. Two shots broke this.
- **Make anything the story needs seen large enough to read on a phone**: at least about 120 px. The fog bank and the small ghost storms of the first film were too small and had to be overlaid in 2D, or stayed illegible.
- **Light every sequence with a warm key, explainer and diagram shots too.** The map-as-terrain sequence went cold and grey for 15 seconds and needed a per-shot grade. The colour band applies to whole scenes with characters; inserts, night and storm may sit outside it, but say so on purpose.

## Look specification

- **Put bloom, a gentle S-curve and distance haze into the look specification and into the opening the user approves**, not into a late pass. Version 1 had flat white lamp discs, clipped highlights and low contrast, and every one of those was a known property of the reference.

## Decisions to settle on the opening, with the user

- **Every visible design choice that recurs** (here: a ruled line against a hand-drawn line). Show both variants side by side on the 5 second opening and get one answer before any take is rendered. Changing it during production cost two restarts. If the narration describes the choice in words ("dead straight"), a changed picture leaves the words wrong: re-record those sentences, or accept the mismatch knowingly and tell the user.
- **Caption size.** Thai captions at the reference's point size look about 25 % taller than Latin ones, because of the vowel and tone marks above and below. The first film kept a larger size (45 px text height against 36-37 px in the reference) by the client's choice. Show two sizes on the opening and let the user decide.
- **The voice.** A voice change after production is possible without touching the picture (`references/production.md`), but mouths that follow the narration must be rendered again. Settle it on the opening.

## Production

- **Plan for workers returning early.** Write the sweeper into the main loop from the first lane (`references/production.md`).
- **Plan the claim-file rule into the shared brief from the start**, so extra lanes can be started at any time without stopping anything.
- **Leave time for the polish pass and the two reviews.** In the first film they took longer than the conform and mix: about 2 hours 40 minutes against 35 minutes (that pass also built a background blur the client later had removed; the grade, overlays, mix and reviews alone are shorter).
