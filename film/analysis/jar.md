## 2. Story mechanics
- Hook (0-7 s): one past-tense sentence, "He [odd action] and told [authority] if [condition] run", ends on a laugh. The odd object (bronze ball in dragon mouth) plus a warning with no reason. 3 s of narration is the full action; the laugh lands at 6-7 s.
- Reason withheld: the device is shown (8 dragons, 8 frogs) but never what it senses until 46.5 s (about 72 percent through the clip).
- Mockers: emperor, whole court, then peasants (gate, 27-29), then guards (40-46). Each mock is more local and personal; the last has voiced dialogue.
- Countable escalation: ball 1 (weeks later, nothing), then "a ball dropped, then another, then all eight at once". Plus laugh counter: laughed, laughed even harder, guards laughed.
- False payoff: the 19-22 s ball fell and nothing happened (reads as proof it is a toy). The second false beat is that he predicts doom at night and nothing visible happens, so he is mocked again.
- Twist: next morning the palace is rubble (46.5 s); it only reads as a vindication because the city, not the hill, was hit.
- Reveal: single mechanism, a pendulum hanging inside the jar and eight rods. Visually it is a top-down shot looking straight down the jar with radial rods, then a close action shot of pendulum striking rod, rod snapping dragon jaw. Then scale-up: one ball is a faraway nudge (mountain), all eight is under you (cross-section).
- Ending image: geological cross-section with a glowing crack under the ruined city; last line lands on "underneath them" with the fault line. No moral line.
- Structure: hook 0-7 / setup 7-13 / pride 13-19 / false payoff 19-22 / mock + exile 22-32 / escalation 32-40 / mock 40-46 / twist 46-48 / mechanism 48-58 / scale 58-65.

## 8. The 5 things that make it work
1. Hook is an unexplained odd act plus a warning ("if this falls out, run") and an instant laugh, in 7 s. Reason held until 72 percent.
2. Mockery ladder that gets worse and more personal (court, peasants, guards), each mock turning the hero smaller, so the vindication is bigger.
3. Countable escalation (1 ball, "another", "all eight") that also teaches the code of the device, so the reveal is already half understood.
4. One-visual reveal: top-down pendulum with 8 radial rods then a rod snapping the jaw, then scale-up 1 vs 8 in a ground cross-section; science explained by the picture alone.
5. Non-stop camera plus colour-coded phases (red court, blue night, orange disaster) and word-by-word captions keep a new thing on screen every 1-3 s.
