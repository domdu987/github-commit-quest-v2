## 2. Story mechanics
- Hook (0-5.3 s): one long sentence: [odd action] + [danger already in the frame] + [sensory proof "hear the pirates laughing"]. Longer than BELL's hook (5 s vs 3 s), but still one thought.
- Reason withheld until 42.9 s (66 % of runtime). The chef answers with a point and "trust the oil" - an anti-explanation. The viewer is given the premise ("oil calms waves") only after the biggest threat is on screen.
- Mockers: the captain (twice, screaming), the pirate captain (cannonball line), the pirates laughing. The chef is silent/deadpan - the "calm wise hero".
- Countable escalation: (1) pirate ship behind -> (2) grappling hook -> (3) storm waves taller than the mast -> (4) pirates swing in to board -> (5) the wave big enough for both ships. Plus the repeated pouring (pour x5 visible).
- False payoff: 22-24 s, captain steers into the storm and the pirates cheer, the audience thinks "they are doomed" and the pirates seem to be winning.
- Reveal: ONE visual mechanism: a bowl of soup, oil spots stay smooth when blown on, then the storm shot reuses the glowing yellow patch on the sea. The mechanism is explained with a second-person real-life analogy.
- Ending image: giant curling wave crashes on the pirate deck (explosion + floating hats). Final frame at 64 s is the explosion with a spray of tricorn hats.
- Second-person: "you're making soup?" (in quote), "Ever blown on a bowl of soup?", "your breath can't grab them", "You see,".
- Structure ratio: hook+context 0-12 s, escalation 13-42 s, reveal 43-55 s, double payoff 55-64 s.

## 8. Five things that make it work
1. A hook with a built-in contradiction: calm hero doing a ridiculous chore while the threat is audible behind him.
2. The anti-explanation: the hero never explains ("trust the oil"), so the viewer waits 43 s for the reason.
3. Escalation by countable physical threats (pirate ship -> hook -> mast-high waves -> boarding -> a wave to swallow both) with colour dropping from sunny to storm-night each step.
4. A reveal built on ONE everyday visual (blowing on soup) that the audience can verify at home, delivered in direct second person, then immediately re-applied on the storm shot.
5. A double payoff: hero lifted safely, then the villains crushed in the final 4 s with the only orange explosion in an all-blue frame.
