## 2. Story mechanics
- Hook (0-3 s): "He [did a weird technical thing] [countable specific number]." 8 words, no stake explained. The 39 feet number is the hook, not the person.
- Reason withheld until 54 s (83 percent). The sentence "his gaps shrink in summer" is told at 38.7 s but the cause (heat expansion) is never named; the audience works it out from the glowing rails.
- Mockers: boss (anger, then smug), rival company/passengers, newspapers/public. The boss is a recurring comic antagonist, with 3 states: purple fury, grim grey, smug grin.
- Countable escalation: gap sizes - finger-wide, coin (May), sheet of paper (July), closed (hottest day). Dual track: perfect railroad "groaning" then kicks. Time marker also counts: May, July, hottest day.
- False payoff: 42-47 s, the boss is thrilled, and tells the hero "even your gaps know you were wrong" (misreads the shrinking as a defect that fixes itself). Second false payoff at 26-35 s: his line is mocked in the press and everyone switches.
- Reveal: one visual mechanism, rails changing colour by heat (purple, pink, orange, yellow-white) and growing until the gap is gone. The same colour language is used on both railroads, so the contrast reads in 2 seconds.
- Ending image: train on the S-curved rails falling, explosion cloud. Last line ends on "rolls onto it", no moral, no summary. The hero's face (shock) appears in the last frame at 64.5.
- Interplay: 2 railways, 2 bosses; the hero is pitted against both, and the underdog wins through physics, unseen by anyone.

## 8. The 5 things that make it work
1. Hook is a bizarre technical act with a precise number ("every 39 feet"); the audience wants the reason and it is withheld for 54 s.
2. A comic antagonist boss whose emotional state is visible in colour (purple, grey, grinning); he is the engine of every beat; the public and rival pile on.
3. Countable gap ladder (finger, coin, paper, closed) with calendar markers (May, July, hottest day), shown by macro shots and calipers, with a false payoff when the boss is happy.
4. Reveal by image only: heat-coloured rails push together; the same colour language later makes the perfect railroad buckle like a snake; the final image is a disaster with the hero reacting.
5. Constant camera motion (dives, whips, dolly, macro to aerial), saturated orange-blue, and a new framing every 1.6 s hold attention with word-by-word captions.
