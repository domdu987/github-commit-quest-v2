## 2. Story mechanics
- Hook (0-4.3 s): "It's impossible to [common thing] in Japan, but companies found something way worse." 13 words, a rule plus a "but... way worse" that promises a horror without naming it. The visual (FIRE button) states the rule and the hammer suggests violence.
- Reason withheld: the mechanism (labor law lock, loophole) is held until 49.2, 81% into the story. Until then the viewer sees a "perk" that seems nice (paid to do nothing).
- Who mocks: the boss, always with a closed-eye teeth grin, giving an empty gift box, the Friday visit. Co-workers silently stare. The happy man in the corner is the false ally.
- Countable escalation: paradise (1) -> coworkers stare (2) -> emails stop (3) -> desk replaced (4) -> Friday boss visits (5) -> happy man vanishes (6). Each step 2-5 s.
- False payoffs: "paid for this?" (19.7) and "At first it's paradise", then "fine. You kick your feet up" (40.6) with drinks and umbrellas, a second relaxing high just before the drop.
- Reveal: one visual mechanism = the fire alarm locked by a "LABOR LAW" padlock, then the panel of labelled switches; the boss flips WORK and FRIENDS off. It is shown during the final 10 s. Last line is the thesis ("nobody can do nothing forever").
- Ending image: the door sign "CAREER DEVELOPMENT ROOM" with the worker visible through the glass, sweating and wide-eyed; slow push in. It loops to the door in the opening act (7.0 s). No resolution.
- Second person: you/your throughout, a literal "Imagine your boss..." at 4.5.
- Counts: 208 words, about 17 sentences, 12 words average; shortest "Just sit here until 6." (5 words).

## 8. The 5 things that make it work
1. Hook "impossible... but way worse" with a literal visual (the FIRE button and the hammer), so the viewer wants the "worse" thing.
2. The second-person experience ("Imagine your boss...", "you kick your feet up"), a simple 6-step slide from paradise to dread with concrete, drawn evidence at each step (cobweb inbox, someone else's photos, a stranger at your desk, the boss's Friday visit).
3. The twist is staged: the happy man is introduced early, believed to be fine (looks happiest in the building), then removed (empty desk with two coffee rings), so the horror lands without any violence.
4. The reveal is a visual metaphor, a locked fire alarm labelled "LABOR LAW" plus a switch panel labelled WORK / FRIENDS, which makes the abstract law concrete and takes only about 10 s.
5. Colour grading follows the story (bright office, mint room, dusk, deep blue ending) with a constantly moving camera and a recurring grinning boss, closing on a single image (the door/window with the worker inside) that echoes the room introduced at 7.0.
