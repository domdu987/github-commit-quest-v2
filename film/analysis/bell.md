## 2. Story mechanics
- Hook (0-3 s): one sentence, past tense, subject-less "They" + shock verb ("buried alive") + odd object ("a bell tied to his finger"). No question, no "you". Premise given in full, mystery is WHY the story goes wrong.
- Withheld reason: the hero smirks in the opening, the doctor says he is dead, the wife is "in the dark with a shovel" - viewer is asked why she does not dig. The answer (fake death + insurance-style debt escape) comes at 37 s (62 % through), then a second micro-twist at 46 s (she chose NOT to dig).
- Who mocks: the neighbour ("probably the wind"), the porch crowd ("You go check!"), the doctor (certified dead). Skeptics, not villains.
- Countable escalation: ring (1) wind -> faster -> whole street -> doctor -> (fast, slow, rare, stop). Time of night stamp "2 in the morning". Code "three short, one long" is a second countable.
- False payoff: doctor "He's dead. I signed the paper myself." The bribe is visible on the certificate ($20) only in hindsight.
- Reveal: not a single visual mechanism - spoken ("he was never dead") plus a cold->warm colour flip and a prop (code card on the wall at 34.5 s). Then the real sting is the ending (widow chooses the house).
- Ending image: widow at dawn leaning on the shovel next to the mound, IOUs buried, bell on post behind her.
- Second-person: only embedded in quotes ("You go check!") and the two "You see," phrases. Narrator never says "imagine".
- Voice: dry, deadpan; humour from the understatement of the neighbour and the doctor.

## 8. Five things that make it work
1. A one-sentence hook that states the entire shocking premise and a single object (bell on a finger) that is the clip's motif - the bell appears in almost every phase.
2. A real-world "weird fact" opening ("You see," explanation) that is then flipped by a deadpan twist: the "victim" is the scammer, the wife is the real winner.
3. Withheld reason with visible clues (smirk, $20 in the certificate, shovel by the door, code card), so the 37 s reveal feels fair and re-watchable.
4. Colour grading carries the story: cold blue night = mystery, warm lanterns = neighbours, sudden bright daylight = truth, sunrise pink = the cynical payoff.
5. Constant camera motion on a 3D set (dive through grave, out the window, along the street) so a very small cast of flat puppets never feels static.
