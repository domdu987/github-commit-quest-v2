## Story (told as a tale based on real practice)
A small boy, the youngest miner, carries a canary in a little cage down a British coal mine and tells everyone: if it stops singing, run.
Underground there is invisible, odourless gas, poisonous and flammable. The foreman trusts only his flame lamp: if the flame burns bright,
it is safe. He laughs ("a bird to sing us lullabies, kid? my lamp is still bright!"); the miners nickname the boy "ไอ้เด็กเลี้ยงนก".
Countdown: day 1 the bird sings a long song; day 5 the song is shorter; day 10 only three notes, then silence — the boy shouts to run;
the foreman lifts his lamp: still bright, "your bird is just sleepy". Next day the bird sings one note and drops to the floor of the cage.
The boy grabs the cage and drags everyone toward the shaft while miners mock him; the flame of the foreman's lamp ignites the gas and a
fireball races down the tunnel behind them (no casualties shown). Reveal: a bird breathes many times faster than a man, the gas reaches it
minutes earlier; the lamp tells only when it is too late. The song getting shorter each day was the gas rising each day. Ending: at the
pithead the boy gives the bird oxygen from a small resuscitation box; it opens its eyes and sings one more note.
