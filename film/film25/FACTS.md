# Film 25 "ไอ้ถังน้ำ": Britain's first tank (series "one amazing war weapon per country", episode 2)

## Facts (checked 2026-11-27)
| claim in the film | status | source |
|---|---|---|
| WWI Western Front by 1915: both sides dug in; trenches, barbed wire and machine guns made crossing no-man's-land deadly | fact | general history |
| Britain (Landships Committee, set up by the Admiralty in 1915) secretly developed armoured "landships" | fact | Tank Museum, "The First Tanks" |
| For secrecy the machines were called "water carriers"; workers at Foster's were told they were water tanks for Mesopotamia; in Dec 1915 Swinton and Lt-Col Dally Jones chose the codename "tank", and it stuck | fact (details disputed: d'Eyncourt proposed "water carrier" on 4 Nov 1915; "tank" chosen 24 Dec 1915; some say workers' slang) | the-past.com "War of Words – Tank"; World of Tanks "100 years of tanks: water carriers"; Great War Forum |
| Mark I weighed about 28 tonnes | fact | Wikipedia "British heavy tanks of WWI" |
| Rhomboid hull with tracks running all the way round so the long track nose could reach the far side of a trench and climb out | fact (Mark I crossed about 3.5 m / 11.5 ft trenches; in Jan 1916 trials "Mother" crossed a 9 ft trench) | Spartacus "Little Willie"; Wikipedia |
| Riveted steel plates; "Male" tanks carried two 6-pounder guns in side sponsons (plus machine guns); "Female" machine guns only | fact | Wikipedia Mark I |
| Sponsons were removed for rail transport; tanks travelled covered by tarpaulins | fact | Wikipedia Mark I |
| A senior commander mocked it at the trial | fact in spirit: at the 2 Feb 1916 Hatfield demonstration Lord Kitchener called it "a pretty mechanical toy" | Swinton's memoirs, via Spartacus / Great War Forum |
| First used at Flers-Courcelette, 15 Sep 1916 | fact | Tank Museum "Tanks at Flers" |
| 49 tanks earmarked; only 32 reached the start line, the rest broke down or ditched (18 went into action) | fact | Tank Museum "Tanks at Flers"; Google Arts & Culture "The First Tank Battle" |
| Machine-gun bullets could not get through the armour; wire was flattened; German defenders were shocked and some fled | fact (period accounts; splash from bullet hits did hurt crews, not shown) | Tank Museum; IWM "Voices of the First World War: tanks on the Somme" |
| An RFC airman reported "a tank is walking up the High Street of Flers with the British Army cheering behind it" (tank D17 "Dinnaken") | fact (wording varies by source) | Tank Museum "Tanks at Flers"; Peter Hart, The Somme |

## Invented (must be marked in the caption)
- The general who mocks it, his lines ("ของเล่นชัดๆ", "ถังน้ำรั่วหมดแล้ว"), his promise to walk behind its backside cheering, and his being in the crowd behind the tank in Flers. (Real echo: Kitchener's "pretty mechanical toy". Kitchener died in June 1916, before Flers, so the general is NOT Kitchener.)
- The nickname "ไอ้ถังน้ำ" as camp slang (the real codename was "tank" = water tank).
- The part-by-part build-up order is condensed for storytelling; the stencilled crate is a dramatisation of the secrecy shipping.
- All characters are cartoon puppets; the comic flight of German soldiers is shown without injury.
