## Story
Old Japan. A young carpenter's apprentice builds a five-storey wooden pagoda whose central pillar (shinbashira) HANGS from the top on a
chain, floating one palm above the ground; the storeys are stacked loosely, not nailed. He says: when an earthquake comes, run INTO the
pagoda. The town's master builder builds a tall rigid tower, every joint nailed tight, "hard as rock", and laughs at the pillar that holds
nothing. Year 1: small quake, the master's tower stands still, the pagoda sways one hand-span (floors swaying opposite ways) -> the town
nicknames it "เจดีย์เมา" (the drunk pagoda). Year 2: pagoda sways two spans; the tower gets a hairline crack at a joint, the master plasters it.
Year 3: three spans; the crack is wide enough for a finger; plastered again. The master gloats. One night the strongest quake ever: the
apprentice runs through town shouting for people to shelter in the pagoda (they mock him); the rigid tower snaps in the middle like a ruler
and collapses (no casualties shown); the pagoda sways like a snake and does not fall. Reveal: the "drunk" sway was it dancing with the quake;
loose storeys sway opposite ways and absorb the shaking, the hanging pillar swings like a pendulum and holds it. Ending: morning, the master
stands on the pile of timber looking at the pillar still gently swinging inside the pagoda.
