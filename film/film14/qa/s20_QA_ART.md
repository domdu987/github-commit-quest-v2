## Verdict: FIX-FIRST
The second half works: shot 22 (big hive jolts, bees pour out) and shots 23-24 (swarm on the eye and trunk, warm hut practicals
behind) are the best frames of the film so far and read on a phone. The first half, which carries the mechanism, does not:
- Shot 20: we see the elephant for ~0.5 s with a tin roof cutting across her body and the wire at her face, then a slide along a
  wire whose hives are ~12 px on the phone. "One wire threads every hive" is not shown.
- Shot 21: the camera sits under the roofs; the hives are cut at the bottom edge and the roofs fill the frame (w.jpg). On the phone
  f010-f020 look identical: no swing wave, no knocking.
So the chain "one push -> whole row swings -> bees burst" jumps from an elephant to bees, and the swarm looks random, not caused.

## Changes (by impact)
1. **Shot 21 (g1368-1452): show the row swing.** Camera at hive height (~1.1 m), outside the roof line, looking along the row so 5-6
   hives are fully in frame and the nearest is >= 200 px wide; roofs only in the top ~20 %. Exaggerate the swing (+-20-25 deg) with a
   clear phase delay running away from the camera and a visible knock (hive edges touching, a 1-frame jolt + dust puff at each knock).
2. **Shot 20 (g1307-1367): start on the contact point, then the wire.** Open on her chest pressing the wire (wire at chest height, no
   roof crossing her; move her or hide the u4 roof), hold ~0.4 s, then slide along the wire at hive height so 3 hives pass large
   (>= 150 px) with the bowed wire clearly threading each hive's bar. Tilt down so sky <= 25 %.
3. **Shots 23-24: act the attack.** Flare the ears and widen the eye (pupil small, brows up) at the swarm hit ~g1510; add a head
   shake / swat (matriarch_swatted) before 1599. In 24 keep the trunk-up readable: fewer bees on the trunk silhouette itself, more as
   a stream along it, so the raised trunk + open mouth reads as a trumpet. Truck right ~0.5 m in 23 so less ear, more face.
4. **Night grade of the hive roofs (20-22):** pull the roof undersides down ~35-40 % and cool them so they sit in the night like the
   board's NIGHT panel and match s15; keep the warm glow for the huts and the bees.
