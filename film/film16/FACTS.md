# Film 16 "ไอ้บ้าลวด" — fact check (Samuel Morse's Washington–Baltimore telegraph line, 1843-1845)

Researched by web search before writing (2026-11-19).

## True facts used in the script
| Script claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Morse was a painter | Samuel F. B. Morse was a portrait painter before the telegraph | Smithsonian "Samuel Morse's Reversal of Fortune"; National Geographic 2016 |
| While painting away from home his wife fell ill; by the time the letter came she was already buried | Feb 1825: Morse was in Washington painting Lafayette; Lucretia died in New Haven; the news came by letter and she was buried before he got home | National Geographic 2016; Mental Floss "The tragic event that led Samuel Morse to develop the telegraph" |
| He wanted news to travel along a wire instantly | (Sources differ on how directly the loss inspired him; the script only says he *wanted* it, which is uncontested) | same |
| He asked Congress for money; a congressman mocked it by proposing half the money go to a hypnotist (mesmerist) | Feb 1843: Rep. Cave Johnson (Tennessee) moved that half of the $30,000 go to experiments in mesmerism; 22 voted for it, it failed | erenow "Lightning Man" ch.10 (Silverman); The Hill is Home "Lost Capitol Hill: Telegraphs in the Capitol" |
| He got the money only narrowly | The bill passed the House 89-83; signed 3 March 1843 ($30,000) | Wikipedia "Baltimore–Washington telegraph line"; LoC Morse Papers "Invention of the telegraph" |
| He first buried the wire in lead pipe underground; dug about 15 km | Ezra Cornell's plow laid wire in lead pipe; about 9-10 miles (~15 km) were laid before it was stopped | Cornell Library exhibit "The Telegraph (1843-1844)"; WETA blog 2024 |
| The buried wire failed: insulation ruined, more than half the money gone | Defective insulation (and pipe heat charring it); ~$23,000 of $30,000 spent (WETA: "half of his appropriation already spent") | LoC Morse Papers; Cornell exhibit; usgennet "Morse's first telegraph line"; WETA |
| He pulled the wire out and strung it on poles | Spring 1844 overhead line on poles, Washington–Baltimore | Cornell exhibit; Wikipedia line article |
| Glass, because electricity cannot pass through glass; beer-bottle necks on the poles | Alonzo Cornell (quoted by Ward): "Prof. Morse and the writer settled on glass insulators, and for economy obtained gratis the necks of beer and porter bottles"; soon replaced by Cornell's knob pattern. Other accounts describe glass plates. Glass is an insulator; wet wood leaks current | National Insulator Association "Glass insulators... the beginnings" (McDougald ch.1); insulators.info |
| On the party convention day in Baltimore the nominee's name reached Washington by wire about an hour before the train | 1 May 1844, Whig convention nominated Henry Clay; Vail at Annapolis Junction wired it to Morse at the Capitol; it arrived 64-75 min before the train | Wikipedia line article; Senate.gov "Morse's telegraph in the Capitol"; OUP blog 2008 |
| Two years later that congressman became the head of the Post Office and the line was put under him | Cave Johnson became Postmaster General in March 1845 (Polk); Congress placed the line under the Postmaster General's supervision (March 1845, $8,000), opened to the public 1 April 1845 | Wikipedia "Cave Johnson"; USPS "The Telegraph" PDF; USPS PMG Johnson bio |

## Invented (comic, does not contradict checkable facts)
- The mocker's promise "ถ้าลวดของเอ็งส่งข่าวได้จริง ข้าจะมานั่งเฝ้ามันเอง" — invented quote (series convention). The mocker is not named on screen; he is "ส.ส.คนหนึ่ง". The payoff (he ended up in charge of the line) is true.
- The nickname "ไอ้บ้าลวด" — invented; Morse was widely ridiculed in Congress (laughter during the 1843 debate is documented).
- The mocker's shout "ลวดของเอ็งตายอยู่ใต้ดินแล้ว" at the failure — invented.
- Visual: the final image of the mocker sitting beside the telegraph instrument is comic invention (as Postmaster General he did supervise the line).
- "ติ๊ด ติ๊ด แต่" — sound word for the clicking instrument.

## Things deliberately NOT claimed
- Not "Morse invented the code by counting printer's type" (Vail story, poorly sourced).
- Not that bottle necks were on the whole 60 km (sources say used early, soon replaced).
- Not the exact minutes (sources: 64 vs 75); the script says "about an hour" / "ก่อนรถไฟตั้งชั่วโมง" (both ≥ 60 min).
