## Story chain on a phone (360x640)
- Hook bottle on the pole (s01): PARTLY. The green glinting thing reads, but it overlaps Morse's head for most of the take and looks like a mint jelly blob, not a beer bottle. A blind viewer may not name it.
- Train + promise (s02-s03): YES for s03 (train, grin, pointing). In s02 the bottle caps on the poles are about 10 px on a phone.
- Silent key (s15): NO. Morse is never seen pressing the key, the register is a blue cylinder at the frame edge, and the workers do not lean in.
- Broken pipe leaking (s16): PARTLY. Torn pipe reads. The leak reads as WATER dripping (cyan drops), not electricity escaping.
- Empty bag (s17): NO. A small limp sack with a white tail hangs at arm's length. It is not turned out, so "empty" does not read.
- Mocker's taunt from the buggy (s18): LATE. For about 3 s of the 4.2 s the mocker is a 90 px figure at the left edge behind tall grass. He only reads in the last second, laughing, not taunting toward the furrow or Morse.

## Per take

### s01 hook — FIX
Good: low angle, ladder rails FG, glint on the bottle, push-in, satisfied grin at the end.
Problems: the first second is a wide, about 60 % sky, with Morse tiny. From f005 on, the bottle sits directly behind or against his head, so the silhouettes merge. The bottle is a translucent mint blob, not a bottle. The wire is never seen draped over the glass.
Fixes:
1. Separate bottle and head. Slide the camera or place Morse about 0.5 D lower and left of the pole top so the capped bottle stands against clean sky, with at least 40 px of air around it at 720 wide, for the whole second half.
2. Make it read as a beer bottle: deeper bottle-green, denser glass with a bright rim highlight, clear neck, shoulder and body. Keep the glint on the neck.
3. Show the beat: in the last 0.6 s the wire visibly drops onto the glass neck (a small bounce). In the opening wide, push in faster, or put a second capped pole in the frame so the strange object reads on frame 0.

### s02 poles + Morse — FIX
Good: the whip arrival, the receding line of poles, the train and farm behind, the long shadows.
Problems: the lower 40 % is empty, soft-textured ground, which reads as blur on a phone. Morse is small. The bottle caps are tiny.
Fixes:
1. Tilt up or raise the horizon so the ground is 25 % or less. Put a FG crate of bottles or rail ties 1-2 D from the lens in the lower third.
2. Bring the nearest capped pole closer, 2-3 D from the lens, so one bottle neck is 60 px or more and the rest repeat into the distance.
3. Medium framing on Morse, head 170 px or more, pointing along the line of poles.

### s03 train + promise — PASS (notes)
Train passes behind, the smoke chain is lovely, the grin reads, the push-in lands. Notes, not blocking: the pole runs straight up behind his head, giving a pole-out-of-head tangency in f005-f008. Slide the camera about 40 px so the pole clears his head. The plan's "taps the wire" is not shown, because the wire is off-frame top right. A hand on the wire would tie the promise to the line.

### s15 silent key — FIX
Problems: Morse stands beside the crate and is never seen pressing the key. The dark crate block fills the lower left 45 %. The register is a blue cylinder clipped at the edge. The workers stand rather than lean in. The silence has no visual anchor.
Fixes:
1. Restage around the key. Morse's finger on the key lever in the frame centre: press, hold, release twice. The register with its idle tape strip is fully in frame next to it, and the tape does not move.
2. Lower the crate in frame, or raise the camera, so the crate top (key + register) is the FG prop, not a dark wall. Add a warm lantern on the crate for a pool of light against the dusk.
3. Two workers bend toward the register with heads 160 px or more, eyes on it. Morse's face goes from hopeful (before pressing) to worried (after). Right now he is sad from frame 1.

### s16 broken pipe — FIX (reveal shot)
Good: a real 3D cut-away, torn wrapping, the split lead pipe, the dive in.
Problems: the leak is cyan droplets falling with gravity, which reads as water, not current. The copper wire has a wood-grain texture and looks like a dowel. The lower 30 % is featureless brown soil with a soft hotspot. The first 0.5 s is a flat orange ground plane.
Fixes:
1. Make the leak electric: jagged, flickering blue-white sparks with a white core and blue halo, which crawl from the torn spots into the soil and light it in blue pools (an emissive soil glow). No falling teardrops.
2. Re-texture the wire as copper: metallic orange, specular, no wood grain. Make the cloth wrap visibly wet and dark.
3. Fill the lower soil band with damp detail (stones, roots, wet dark patches lit by the sparks), or frame tighter so the pipe sits lower. Shorten the flat top-plane opening.

### s17 empty bag — FIX
Problems: the bag hangs limp at arm's length, small, near the right edge, with a white tail that reads as an animal tail. It is never turned out, so "empty" does not read on a phone. The workers do not lean on shovels as a reaction.
Fixes:
1. The beat itself: Morse holds the bag upside down at chest height, centre frame, and shakes it. The lining flaps out and a single coin, or nothing but a moth or dust puff, drops. Bag 150 px or more.
2. Remove or shorten the white tail on the bag (the drawstring) so it reads as a money bag (same prop as s12).
3. Workers in the background visibly slump onto their shovels on the shake (a dip of 0.1 D), so the reaction reads in the frame.

### s18 mocker's taunt — FIX (false-payoff quote)
Problems: during most of the quote (38.46-40.80) the mocker is about 90 px at the far left edge behind huge FG grass. The mule is cut by the right frame edge, with no head. The close at the end has flat untextured red and blue boxes for the buggy. He laughs straight to camera instead of leaning out toward the furrow or Morse. The furrow ("dead under the ground") is never in the shot.
Fixes:
1. Medium-low on the mocker from the start of the quote: head 200 px or more, centre-left, buggy seat and wheel as FG, with the furrow or the line visible on the side he points to. Lip-sync must be legible for the whole quote, not only the last second.
2. Acting: he leans out over the buggy side and points down at the furrow on "ใต้ดินแล้ว", belly bouncing about 0.06 D at about 3 Hz, laugh tears. Then the quick push-in on that word.
3. Dress the buggy: textured wood seat and side panels instead of flat red and blue blocks. Keep the whole mule in frame or clearly out of it, not a headless body at the edge. Thin the FG grass so it frames rather than covers.

### s22 glass answer — PASS, provisional (re-shot in progress; notes for the re-shoot)
The bottle lift and glint read instantly and the joy reads. Notes: the first second is Morse behind a flat, untextured orange wagon wall about 45 % of the frame high. Lower it or texture it with planks and the crate of bottles. Keep the lifted bottle fully inside the frame, because it is cropped at the right edge in f005-f012. Have Morse look up at the bottle (eye-line) at the glint, then smile.

### s23 bottle capped, callback — FIX
Same camera and marks as s01, as planned. It inherits all of s01's problems: the bottle merges with his head, the jelly-blob bottle, no wire on the glass. The pole is a pinkish salmon colour instead of the dark, rain-wet wood the AFTER_RAIN light needs.
Fixes: 1-3 as s01, applied identically so the rhyme holds. Also darken and wet the pole with a specular sheen and a couple of drips, matching s20/s24.

### s24 reveal: pulse does not leak — FIX (reveal shot)
Good: a clean whip along the wire, the pulse glow, the settle on the bottle.
Problems: the mechanism contrast is missing. The pole below is bright salmon and dry, not wet and dark, and there is no reminder of the sparks that crawled down the wet wood in s20. The whole bottle glows cyan, which reads as "the glass soaks it up". The outgoing wire stays dark, so the message seems to stop. About 60 % of the frame is empty sky.
Fixes:
1. Wet, dark pole with drips and a puddle at the foot. As the pulse crosses the neck, show a weak spark trying to creep down, stopping at the glass rim and dying. Keep the pole dark.
2. Light only the neck and rim briefly as the pulse passes, then send the pulse on along the outgoing wire. The message continues, and the glass only blocks the way down.
3. Show the context: frame lower or add the next poles with bottles receding, plus wet grass and puddles, so the sky is 40 % or less.

### s30 ending — FIX
Good: night chamber, chandelier, tape loops on the floor, the yawn + Z + mail-bag sash read in the close, Morse grinning at the door (head about 90 px in the close).
Problems: the pull-back ends on the cut frame with the mocker at about 80 px and Morse about 45 px (about 22 px on a phone). The register sits far behind him on the table instead of beside him, so "he guards the wire himself" depends on the tape alone. The room is lit almost evenly, without candle-pool night contrast. The tape loops read like a toy train track at phone size.
Fixes:
1. Stop the pull-back at a medium-wide: mocker head 150 px or more, register on a small table right beside his stool, clicking, with tape spooling out of it onto his boots. Morse at the door is 70 px or more and is the last thing the eye finds. Cut on "เอง" there.
2. Night light: drop the ambient light about 40 %. The key light is the candle in his hand plus a warm pool on the register. The doorway backlights Morse, and the windows are deep blue. Depth comes through light, not even exposure.
3. Tape: narrower, paper-white, more crumpled, piling higher round his boots and spilling off the register. Drop the even loops that read as rails.
