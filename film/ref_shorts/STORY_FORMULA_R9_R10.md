# Story formula of R9 (sniper) and R10 (Tiger vs tractor)
Transcripts: R9_transcript.txt, R10_transcript.txt

1. **Hook (0-5 s):** a paradox in one line. "Deadliest sniper... never used a scope." / "Shuffled three tanks like a deck of cards, while his neighbour polished a Tiger."
2. **Underdog (5-13 s):** the hero is plain or small (a 5-ft farmer; a plain T-34). The rival is fancy (scope; Tiger).
3. **"You see..." plus mockery (13-21 s):** the rival laughs: "Son, real snipers use a scope." / "Is that a tank or a tractor?" The hero says nothing.
4. **The method, told in concrete details and numbers:** -40°, snow in his mouth, 4 shots and 4 drop, 25 men; a mile away, shells bounce like peas, rings on the barrel.
5. **The rival escalates or gets overconfident:** shells every hill, 50 shells only hit the coat / "they'll never learn".
6. **Showdown or turn:** a real sniper with a scope and the sunset flash / a wheel cracks and nine others must come off.
7. **Explainer metaphor (the lesson):** "a scope is a tiny mirror" / pocket watch vs hammer.
8. **Callback punchline:** turns the mockery around. "Got a spare?" "Sorry, it only fits tractors." / "And that's why..." loops back to the start.

## Visual rules
- One short sentence per shot. Cut every 1-3 s.
- Every noun is shown literally (thermometer at -40, map, newspaper, tape measure, pocket watch).
- Two rival characters stay recognisable all the way through.
