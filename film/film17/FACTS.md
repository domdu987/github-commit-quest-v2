# Film 17 "ไอ้ช่างวัดหู" — fact check (Alphonse Bertillon and the first anthropometric identification, Paris 1882-1883)

Researched by web search before writing (2026-11-19).

## True facts used in the script
| Script claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A clerk at the Paris police measured every arrested man, including the ear, with steel calipers | Bertillon was a low-ranking records clerk at the Paris Prefecture of Police; his system measured bony body parts (head length and width, left middle finger, left foot, left forearm, ear...) with calipers; he catalogued ear shapes in detail | banditsroost "Does he measure up" (2026); crimeandpsychology substack "The incredible and sad tale of grumpy Alphonse" (2024); Wikipedia "Alphonse Bertillon"; NIH NLM "Visible Proofs: Alphonse Bertillon" |
| Paris, about 140 years ago | Trial period Nov/Dec 1882 – Feb 1883 (≈143 years before 2026) | crimeandpsychology; tracesdefrance.fr "Alphonse Bertillon: the father of criminal identification" |
| Repeat offenders gave false names and got a first-offender's sentence | Repeat offenders faced harsher penalties; they evaded them by giving aliases, and police could not reliably recognise them | Wikipedia "Alphonse Bertillon"; NIH Visible Proofs; Mental Floss (2014, now offline) |
| Tens of thousands of criminal photos, useless when a man grew a moustache | The Prefecture held tens of thousands of cards and about 80,000 photographs that could not be searched; beards/moustaches defeated recognition (Bertillon later insisted on a profile photo because it "could not be disguised by moustaches/beards") | crimeandpsychology ("80 000 photographs"); banditsroost |
| Colleagues laughed at him | "How the others laughed at poor old Bertillon as he meticulously recorded the criminals' foot length... Oh yes, how they laughed"; another account: jeers from prisoners and police officers at La Santé | crimeandpsychology; forensicfield.blog |
| The police chief gave him only three months to catch one repeat offender | The new Prefect (Jean Camecasse) gave him three months (late 1882 – Feb 1883): "If during this period your method fishes up a single recidivist..." | crimeandpsychology; tracesdefrance.fr; banditsroost |
| 11 measurements per man, written on a card, filed in drawers by size | "eleven measurements of parts of the body which Bertillon believed never changed"; card files classified by each measurement in small/medium/large groups so one card could be found among thousands | banditsroost; Wikipedia "Bertillon system" (classification) |
| Two months with no repeat offender; success in the final week | "success came during the final week of his trial period" | banditsroost |
| A man said his name was Dupont; the card said Martin; earlier arrest for stealing empty bottles | Feb 1883: a man arrested as "Dupont" was identified by his measurements as "Martin", arrested earlier; "arrested ... for the not-very-heinous crime of stealing an empty bottle" | crimeandpsychology; banditsroost; (Thorwald, *The Century of the Detective*, 1964, gives the bottle theft and Dec 1882 date) |
| Adult bones do not change | The method relied on bone lengths fixed after growth ends (adults) | banditsroost; Wikipedia |
| The next year he identified 200-odd more repeat offenders | 241 recidivists identified in 1884 (another account says 300) | banditsroost; crimeandpsychology — script says "สองร้อยกว่าคน", true under both |

## Invented (comic, does not contradict checkable facts)
- The mocker "สารวัตรใหญ่" (chief inspector) is a composite stand-in for the colleagues who laughed; unnamed; his quote and promise are invented (series convention).
- The nickname "ไอ้ช่างวัดหู" is invented.
- "Last week" countdown shout by the mocker is invented.
- Ending: the inspector being the first man made to sit for the ear measurement is a comic invented ending.
- "Last month" (เมื่อเดือนก่อน) for Martin's earlier arrest: the earlier arrest was during the trial (Dec 1882 per Thorwald), i.e. weeks before Feb 1883.

## Things deliberately NOT claimed
- Not that Bertillon invented fingerprints (he did not; fingerprints later replaced his system).
- Not the exact date (sources disagree; one gives an impossible "29 Feb 1883").
- Not the mugshot claim in narration (true — he standardised front + profile photos — but cut for pace; may appear visually only).
