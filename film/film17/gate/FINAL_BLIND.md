# Blind viewer notes

1. Hook (0-5s): A clerk puts iron calipers on people's ears. The narrator says he measures every caught thief's ears, one ear at a time. It is odd because nobody measures ears, and the frames show several people with calipers at their heads. The line is clear, but there is no visible reason yet for the ear measuring.

2. Mockery: The chief inspector (big, blue uniform, moustache) laughs at the clerk at about 15-21s. He says measuring ears will never catch an old thief, and that if it does he will sit for the clerk to measure his ear. The whole station then calls the clerk "the ear-measurer" (21s). At about 35-39s the inspector shouts at him that one week is left.

3. Mechanism: He measures 11 body points per thief (head, fingers, foot, arm, ear and so on) and writes them on a card. Cards are filed in drawers sorted by size. A new suspect is measured the same way. The head size picks a drawer, the finger size narrows it to one box, and the ear size narrows it to one card. When all the numbers match, the card shows who the man really is, whatever name he gives. The logic is clear. The funnel (drawer, then box, then card) is only shown quickly.

4. Reveal (about 40-56s): In the last week a man is brought in who says he is "Dupont" and has never been arrested. The clerk measures him and opens the long-head drawer, then the box, then the single card. All measurements match. The card says "Martin", arrested last month for stealing empty bottles. So the liar is caught by his own record. This is shown by the opened drawers and the card, plus the narration. I could not read the card text on screen.

5. Ending (about 62-65s): The inspector's face goes red and he sits stiffly while the clerk measures his ear. He is the first to sit for the measuring, as he promised. The callback to his own boast is the joke, and the red face adds to it.

6. Confusing moments:
- 4.8-11s: the narration covers Paris 140 years ago, fake names and light sentences. The pictures are a generic empty street and small figures, so the text is not illustrated and I did not follow it visually.
- 11-14s: "tens of thousands of photos, a moustache is enough to fool people". The shelves are shown, but no moustache gag.
- 28-34s: the 11-point list is spoken fast. Pictures of conveyor belt, boots, a hand and a card flash by, so I could not match each point.
- 39-44s: the suspect is introduced quickly, and it is hard to tell who he is among the many figures.
- 44-56s: the drawer opening and the match are fast. The "Martin" card is hard to read at phone size.
- 56-62s: "name changes, moustache shaved, adult bones do not change" and "over 200 old thieves caught the next year". The shots are crowded rooms and do not clearly show the success.
- 62-65s: the final sit-down is clear, but the time is short. The punchline lands only on the last frame, with the inspector's face red.
