# Likeness scoring tool

`score.py` measures a clip, a frame folder or a still against the look of the two reference clips and writes a scorecard.

**The index only covers measurable properties (colour statistics, edge density, line widths, camera motion, caption geometry). It is not a perceptual similarity score.** A high index says "nothing that this tool can measure is off"; it does not say "this looks like the reference". A clip of two white discs on a colour card scores 0.86 (see `VALIDATION.md`, check 5). Read the blind spots at the end before trusting a number.

This tool was validated and repaired by a second pass; what was tested, what was wrong and what was changed is in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-github-commit-quest-v2/e8b79665-ba86-5bba-9861-f4e29c068ae7/scratchpad/proj/v2/eval/VALIDATION.md`. The builder's original files are kept in `validation/orig/`.

## Usage

    python3 /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-github-commit-quest-v2/e8b79665-ba86-5bba-9861-f4e29c068ae7/scratchpad/proj/v2/eval/score.py INPUT --out DIR [--label NAME] [--frames A:B] [--no-captions]
    python3 /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-github-commit-quest-v2/e8b79665-ba86-5bba-9861-f4e29c068ae7/scratchpad/proj/v2/eval/score.py --metrics-md        # prints the metric table below

- `INPUT` is an MP4, a folder of frames (`f_0001.png ...`, taken as 30 fps; files that do not match `f_NNNN` are ignored when such files exist) or one still. Frames of another size are resized to 720x1280.
- `--frames A:B` scores frames A to B only (0-based, inclusive).
- `--no-captions` is for renders without burned-in captions: the captions group is left out. Without it a clip with no caption scores 0 on captions, because the reference shows a caption in 96 to 100 percent of its frames.

It writes three files into `DIR`:

| File | Content |
|---|---|
| `scorecard.json` | every metric (value, range used, whole-film range, tolerance, score, verdict, reason if not scored, source), group scores, `index`, `index_strict`, `coverage`, notes, detected transitions and snap moves, unscored measurements, per-frame spread of the picture statistics |
| `scorecard.md` | the readable table per group: metric, our value, reference range, clip A, clip B, score, verdict, source; the index, the strict index and the share of the weight that was measured; notes |
| `report.jpg` | sampled frames with head circles (green = head, red crossed = round pale blob rejected), caption boxes and the y = 880 line; head close-ups with the measured ink edges; the timeline (zoom rate, pan, cuts, dissolves, snap moves, static holds, captions, head presence, blink-like events); caption crops; the metric list |

Run time on this machine with 2 threads: about 90 to 100 s for a 65 s clip, 11 to 12 s for a 5 s clip, under 1 s for a still.

## How a score is formed

- Each metric has a reference range. Inside the range the score is 1; outside it falls linearly and reaches 0 at the stated distance.
- Group score = mean of the metrics of the group that were scored. Index = weighted mean of the groups: characters 0.30, colour and tone 0.15, density and light structure 0.15, camera and edit 0.20, animation life 0.10, captions 0.10, re-normalised over the groups that were measured.
- `coverage` is the share of the weight that was measured. `index_strict` counts a group that the kind of input allows but that could not be measured as 0 (no head found, picture so washed out that the detectors fail). Compare two inputs on the strict index when their "not measured" groups differ.
- **Minimum material.** A metric that needs more material than the input holds is shown with its value and marked "not measured" with the reason: interval between framing changes needs 8 s, mean shot length 15 s, dissolve share 4 transitions, dissolve length 2 dissolves, snap easing 3 snaps, static share, hold, zoom and pan 30 in-shot frames, blink-like events 15 s of tracked heads, face changes 3 s of tracked heads, character motion 30 steady-camera frames with a head, caption duration and hard swap 5 captions, stroke width 200 stroke runs, outline metrics 2 heads with a measurable outline, the characters group a head in 3 sampled frames.
- **Short inputs use piece ranges for four groups.** The ranges in the table below hold for a whole film. For a clip under 7.5 s, 15 s or 30 s (and for a still) a range of the characters, camera, animation or captions group is widened to the 10th to 90th percentile of the non-overlapping 5 s, 10 s or 20 s pieces of the two reference clips (`ref_windows.json`). Such rows are marked "piece range". Colour and density keep the spec targets, which are stated for single interior frames; an exterior or night scene can score low there without being wrong. Scores of short clips scatter in any case: with whole-film ranges, 5 s pieces of the reference itself score an index of 0.81 to 0.96.
- **Plausibility guards.** A set of frames with almost no colour variety (colour entropy under 3.5 bits; reference frames 5.7 to 7.4) is treated as a flat colour card and scores 0 on colour and density. Captions that consist only of plain filled bars score 0. Both are cheap checks against degenerate input, not protection against a determined fake.
- Picture statistics ignore rows 850 to 959, the caption band. They are taken on every 15th frame starting at frame 6 for clips over 300 frames, on every 5th frame for shorter clips; the clip value is the median over those frames.

## Known blind spots

1. **Not perceptual.** Nothing here judges drawing quality, face and costume design, whether the set reads as the same kind of place, texture style, composition, timing against the narration, sound or story. `VALIDATION.md` check 5 shows a clip of two jittering white discs with a plum ring on a noisy colour card that scores 0.86.
2. **Calibrated on two clips.** Most ranges come from the spec; eleven were widened to the tool's own readings (each named in the Source column) and several have no spec number at all and rest on the two clips only (luma thirds, thin lines, thick / thin ratio, ink edge softness, ink chroma, character motion, caption coverage, the piece ranges). The reference scores 1.000 by construction.
3. **Short clips.** Pieces of the reference itself scatter (5 s: index 0.81 to 0.96 with film ranges). A 5 s clip can show that something is clearly off, not that it is right.
4. **Head detector.** Checked by eye on 260 sampled frames: about 98 percent of the marked blobs are heads, about 75 to 80 percent of the clearly visible pale heads are found, 5 percent of the fitted circles are 15 percent or more off in size. Missed: heads in profile or from behind, one eye hidden, under 44 px, tinted beyond saturation 0.30 or darker than 140 (night shots, the purple-faced boss), mostly covered by hat or hair. Accepted wrongly: any pale disc with a dark ring and two dots.
5. **Outline width.** Needs a background lighter than the ink. Thin and thick are the 10th and 90th percentile of the measurable arc. The direction of the thick side is reported, not scored.
6. **Ink colour** shifts a few units between PNG frames and 4:2:0 video, and by 13 to 18 dE between scenes of clip B; the distance to #3a2832 only means something for a whole film, the chroma also for a scene.
7. **Limb width** is a histogram peak under the head that props and set lines can win. Plausibility check only.
8. **Camera.** The motion fit follows the largest textured layer. On a 2D move it is accurate (known 7.7 percent/s read as 7.5, known 27.3 px/s as 27.4). In a 3D scene the zoom rate depends on which depth layer wins: on two windows of clip A a feature-matching check gave a fifth of the tool's value. Parallax itself is not measured.
9. **Transitions.** Wipes, whip pans, flashes and cuts hidden in fast moves are not found, a dissolve during a fast move can be missed, a hidden cut can be labelled a dissolve. The tool counts fewer shots than a viewer (14 of 16 in clip A, 18 of 22 in clip B) and the ranges for shot length, interval and dissolve share are set on its own readings. One dissolve's length is good to about 3 frames (worst 5).
10. **Animation.** The blink-like event rate is not the blink rate: in clip A 3 of its 8 events are blinks and 3 of 7 known blinks are found. The character-motion share says that the character box changes more than a rigid picture under the same camera move would; it cannot tell acting from random jitter, and parallax between figure and set counts as motion.
11. **Captions.** Only white text with a black stroke on one line in rows 836 to 976 is found. The soft shadow around the stroke, the typeface and the text itself are not checked. Words are counted from gaps.
12. **Input assumptions.** 30 fps and 720x1280.
13. **Not checked at all.** Lens and perspective, parallax, depth of field, bloom, shadow colour and softness, set-object count, surface detail on walls and floors, costume colours, mouth sync, walk cycles, dissolve mix curve, motion blur.
