# Film 21 "ไอ้ชาวนาบนเนิน" — fact check (Battle of Stirling Bridge, 11 September 1297)

Researched by web search before writing (2026-11-20). Pages read in full: Wikipedia "Battle of Stirling Bridge", Wikipedia "Hugh de Cressingham",
HistoryNet "Springing the Trap at Stirling Bridge" (James G. Taylor, 2018), Historic Environment Scotland Inventory Battlefield BTL28 (HES).
Most chronicle wording below comes through these secondary pages (Walter of Guisborough, Chronicle of Hemingburgh), not the chronicles themselves.

## True facts used in the script
| Script line | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "เจ็ดร้อยกว่าปีก่อน", Scotland | 11 Sept 1297, near Stirling on the River Forth (729 years before 2026) | Wikipedia; HES BTL28; Britannica |
| English army full of armoured knights; Scots mostly on foot with spears | English had heavy cavalry (HistoryNet "some 200 mail-clad knights" in the vanguard; Guisborough per HES 1,000 horse); Scots had very few horse (Guisborough per HES 180; HistoryNet "three dozen cavalrymen") and fought as spearmen on foot | HistoryNet; HES |
| Scots waited on the hill on the other side of the river | Scots on the north side, on high ground (Abbey Craig, where the Wallace Monument stands; Wikipedia notes Spittal Hill is also argued). English on the south bank | HES; Wikipedia; National Wallace Monument |
| One narrow wooden bridge, two horses fill it | Guisborough: "so narrow that even two horsemen could scarcely and with much difficulty ride side by side" | HistoryNet (quoting Guisborough); HES ("a pair of horsemen could scarcely and with difficulty cross at the same time"); Wikipedia ("only two horsemen cross abreast") |
| Treasurer Hugh de Cressingham, the army's money man, pushed to cross the bridge rather than go round, to save the king's money | Cressingham was Treasurer of the English administration in Scotland (1296-97). Guisborough has him say not "to waste the king's money by vain manoeuvres. So let us cross over right away". Lundie's plan to cross by a ford about two miles upstream was rejected | HistoryNet; HES; Wikipedia (Cressingham; Lundie's ford) |
| The Scots waited while the English crossed | Hemingburgh: the Scots waited until "as many of the enemy had come over as they believed they could overcome" | Wikipedia; HES |
| "ข้ามมาเป็นพัน" (thousands had crossed) | Wikipedia estimates "possibly about 2,000" on the north bank (uncertain); HistoryNet "about half of Surrey's army" | Wikipedia; HistoryNet |
| "ราวครึ่งหนึ่ง" (about half) | HistoryNet: the attack came when "about half of Surrey's army was deployed on the north bank". Wikipedia's ~2,000 differs; "ราว" (roughly) is kept on purpose | HistoryNet |
| Scots rushed down and seized the bridgehead; the crossed men could not retreat, trapped by the river | Scots came down from the high ground, took the end of the bridge; the English were "caught on the low ground in the loop of the river with no chance of relief or of retreat" | HES; Wikipedia |
| The rest could not cross to help, only watch from the other bank | Surrey (Warenne) "had stayed south of the river" and did not send help; he later ordered the bridge destroyed and retreated to Berwick | Wikipedia; HES; HistoryNet |
| Some went back by jumping into the river and swimming | "A few hundred may have escaped by swimming" | Wikipedia; HistoryNet ("some knights took their chances in the Forth") |

## Dramatised / invented (comic, not contradicting checkable facts) — marked in the post caption
- Cressingham's quoted line "ชาวนาบนเนินไม่กี่คน! อ้อมไปเปลืองเงินหลวง ข้ามสะพานนี่แหละ เร็วดี ประหยัดดี!" is a **dramatised paraphrase**. The money-saving part matches Guisborough. The "a few farmers on a hill" mockery is **invented**; no source quotes him mocking the Scots.
- The camp's line "เห็นไหม กลัวจนขยับไม่ได้!" is **invented**.
- The nickname "ไอ้ชาวนาบนเนิน" is **invented**, series convention.
- "ข้ามมาสิบคน... ร้อยคน... เป็นพัน" is a dramatised countdown. Only "thousands by the time of the attack" is backed (≈2,000 per Wikipedia).
- The trumpet ("เสียงแตร") as the attack signal is a **dramatised detail**. The pages read do not say how the attack was signalled.
- The ending gag (swimming back with "ตูม ตูม ตูม") is a comic, bloodless reading of the true escapes by swimming.

## Things deliberately NOT claimed or shown
- **No troop totals.** Sources disagree badly: Wikipedia infobox English 5,300-6,300 vs Scots 9,000; HistoryNet English ~10,000 infantry vs Scots ~8,000; Guisborough 50,000 vs 40,000. The brief says "smaller side", but only the Scots' far smaller cavalry is consistent across sources. The script says only "อัศวินม้าเกราะเต็มทุ่ง" vs "ส่วนใหญ่เดินเท้าถือหอก", and the "ไม่กี่คน" is the mocker's own sneer.
- No deaths, no casualty figures, no drowning shown. Cressingham's real fate (killed, body mutilated) is not mentioned or shown.
- Not that Surrey overslept or knighted squires (true per HistoryNet/HES but HES calls the oversleeping story "remarkably unlikely"). Cut for pace.
- Not "Braveheart" details (no bridge in the film, kilts and woad are anachronistic). Costume: plain tunics and spears for the Scots. No kilts or blue face paint.
- Andrew Moray (co-commander, mortally wounded) is not named, for pace. The Scots are shown as a group under two leaders on the hill.
