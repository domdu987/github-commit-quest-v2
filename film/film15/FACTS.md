# Film 15 "ไอ้ปลาดิ้น" — fact check (Dick Fosbury and the Fosbury Flop)

## True facts used in the script
| Script claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Everyone else rolled over the bar face-down (straddle) in the early 1960s | The straddle/western roll were the standard styles; Fosbury could not master the straddle and kept knocking the bar | Wikipedia "Dick Fosbury"; World Athletics heritage feature |
| USA, 1963, he started jumping backwards | At 16 (1963, Medford, Oregon; Grants Pass meet in May 1963) he developed the back-first technique | Wikipedia; todayifoundout.com |
| A newspaper ran his photo calling him the laziest jumper / like a fish | One newspaper captioned his photo "World's Laziest High Jumper"; the Medford Mail Tribune caption "Fosbury Flops Over Bar" (1964) gave the name, and Oregon Encyclopedia says it compared him to a fish (a "flop" like a fish on a dock) | Wikipedia; Oregon Encyclopedia "Fosbury, Richard" |
| Critics said kids copying him would break their necks | US Olympic coach Payton Jordan warned imitators would "wipe out an entire generation of high jumpers because they all will have broken necks" | History.com "Fosbury flops to an Olympic record"; Oregon Encyclopedia; Telegraph obituary 2023 |
| Five years later, Olympic final (Mexico City 1968) | 20 Oct 1968, Fosbury won gold | Olympedia results 60644; Olympics.com |
| Bar about 30 cm above his head; missed twice, cleared on the third and last attempt | Gold at 2.24 m (Olympic and US record), cleared on the 3rd attempt after two misses; Fosbury was 1.93 m tall (2.24 − 1.93 ≈ 31 cm) | Olympedia; World Athletics feature; Wikipedia |
| Rivals dropped out one by one | Gavrilov out at 2.22 (bronze 2.20), Caruthers failed 2.24 (silver 2.22) | Olympedia / Wikipedia 1968 men's high jump |
| The arched body crosses the bar part by part, the rest hangs below; same push, higher bar | The arch lets the body's centre of mass stay at or below bar level while body parts pass over in sequence, so a flop clears a bar higher than the centre of mass rises | PBS NewsHour "The not-so-hidden physics..."; Popular Science "How high jumping works"; Walker, Flying Circus of Physics |
| Four years later most Olympic jumpers flopped; nobody broke his neck | At Munich 1972, 28 of the 40 jumpers used the flop; "the legions of paralyzed jumpers never materialized" | Oregon Encyclopedia; Olympics.com (13 of 16 finalists by 1980) |
| Sawdust landing pit | Early pits were sawdust/wood chips; Fosbury said landing on sawdust compressed two vertebrae; foam pits came later | Wikipedia; todayifoundout.com |

## Invented (dramatised) parts — checked not to contradict facts
- **"โค้ชรุ่นเก๋า" (the veteran coach)** is a composite of the real critics (Payton Jordan's broken-neck warning, the newspaper caption). He is NOT named, so the film puts no false act on a real person.
- His promise ("if this wins gold I'll do it backwards myself"), his shout after the two misses, and his comic fall into the sawdust pile are invented comedy (allowed: comic invented ending).
- "หนังสือพิมพ์ลงรูป... เหมือนปลาดิ้นบนบก" paraphrases the real caption; "ไอ้ปลาดิ้น" as the crowd's nickname is our Thai rendering of "Flop".
- Picture rules: do NOT show a foam landing mat in the 1963 high-school scenes (show sawdust/wood chips); Mexico 1968 may show a foam pit. Do not show a world-record board (2.24 was an Olympic record, not a world record). No text signs anyway.
