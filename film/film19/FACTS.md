## True facts used in the script
| Script claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| He was a carpenter from the country | "a carpenter by trade who was self-taught in clock making"; WIKI "English joiner and clockmaker", grew up in Barrow upon Humber (Lincolnshire village); his early clocks had wooden movements | RMG; WIKI; SCI |
| England, nearly 300 years ago; at sea nobody knew where the ship was; ships were wrecked | The 1714 Longitude Act was passed because longitude could not be found at sea; the H1 Lisbon voyage (1736) itself shows ships 60 miles off their reckoning | ACT; RMG |
| Parliament offered £20,000 for finding position within half a degree | 1714 Act: £20,000 for half a degree, £15,000 two-thirds of a degree, £10,000 one degree | ACT |
| Astronomers on the Board said the answer was in the Moon | Astronomers (Maskelyne, Bradley) championed the lunar-distance method; Maskelyne tested it on the Barbados trial and became Astronomer Royal and a Commissioner of Longitude | WIKI; RMG |
| First sea clock: five years | WIKI "It took Harrison five years to build his first sea clock" (H1, brought to London 1735) | WIKI; SCI |
| Second, then third: "more than ten years" | H3 was begun 1740; RMG "19 years", WIKI "seventeen years". Script says "สิบกว่าปี" (more than ten), true for both | RMG; WIKI |
| He shrank it into a round watch "the size of a saucer" | H4 is in silver pair cases about 13 cm (5.2 in) across; "looked like a large pocket watch" | WIKI; RMG |
| Noon at sea vs home time: 1 hour difference = 15 degrees | Earth turns 360° in 24 h = 15° per hour; this is the principle of the timekeeper method | standard astronomy; RMG |
| His son carried it to Jamaica | William Harrison took H4 on HMS Deptford, sailed 18 Nov 1761, to Jamaica | WIKI; RMG |
| It was off by only five seconds | At Jamaica H4 was found 5 s slow (≈1.25' of longitude) after allowing for its rate. Historians note the figure depended on the assumed longitude of Port Royal; the 5 s figure is the standard cited one | WIKI; oceannavigator / Gould cited in search |
| The Board said it could be luck and wanted another trial | WIKI: the Board said the accuracy could have been luck and demanded another trial | WIKI |
| Second trial at Barbados: more accurate than the Moon method | 1764: H4 within 39 s, under 10 miles; Maskelyne's lunar distances ~30 miles | WIKI; RMG Barbados blog |
| He got only half the money | 1765: £10,000 on demonstrating the principles; the other £10,000 only once other makers produced similar timekeepers | RMG; WIKI |
| The watch was put in a box with three seals | 5 May 1766 Maskelyne received H4 "locked up in a box sealed with three seals" for testing at Greenwich | RMG |
| Aged nearly 80, the King tested his new clock himself, every day, ten weeks | George III tested H5 "himself at the palace", ten weeks of daily observations, May-July 1772 (Harrison born 1693, so 79) | WIKI |
| Then Parliament paid him | 1773 (age 80) Parliament awarded £8,750, with the King's encouragement; he never received the official full prize (never awarded to anyone) | WIKI; RMG; EBSCO / encyclopedia.com |

## Invented / dramatised (to be stated in the caption)
- **The astronomer-mocker is invented.** He is a composite of the Board's astronomers; he is not named and not Maskelyne.
- His dare "ถ้านาฬิกาของช่างไม้แม่นขนาดนั้น ให้พระราชามาจับเวลาเองเลย!" is invented. The payoff, that the King really timed Harrison's clock himself, is true.
- The nickname "ไอ้ช่างไม้" is invented. Harrison's lack of formal schooling was held against him by the scientific establishment, as all accounts describe.
- The line "ฟลุ๊ค! ต้องลองใหม่!" is put in the mocker's mouth. The Board as a body judged the Jamaica result possibly luck.
- "ยิ่งทำ ผมยิ่งหงอก" is a comic flourish. Decades did pass between H1 (1730-35) and H4 (1759).
- Visuals: the workshop, the ship, the sealed box and the palace are staged, not portraits.

## Things deliberately NOT claimed
- No day count for the Jamaica voyage. Sources give both 81 days and arrival on 19 or 21 Jan 1762.
- No exact H3 year count. Sources say 17 or 19.
- No "By God, Harrison, I will see you righted" quote. It is popular but was not found in the sources checked.
- Not that he "won the Longitude Prize". He received rewards (about £23,065 in total per WIKI), never the official full prize.
- No claim that the King paid. The money came from Parliament.
