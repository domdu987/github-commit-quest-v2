## Story
Boston waterfront 1919. A huge riveted steel tank (about 5 storeys, ~15 m tall, ~27 m wide) holds 9 million litres of molasses; it groans
when filled and molasses seeps from its riveted seams. A boy, the youngest apprentice on the dock, puts a bucket under the drips. The
foreman laughs ("the tank is just sweating, kid") and orders it painted brown so nobody sees the leaks. The boy paints, but secretly keeps
putting the bucket under the drips every morning; the dock nicknames it "ถังเหงื่อแตก" (the sweating tank). All winter nothing happens; the
foreman kicks the bucket away, smug. Then one unusually warm January morning... Mechanism: the tank was never tested (it should have been
filled with water first); the warm molasses expands and pushes on walls that were too thin; the groaning was rivets about to go. At half
past noon rivets shoot out like bullets and a wave of molasses taller than a house floods the street faster than people can run (one
image: the brown wave pouring down the street, no casualties shown). Ending: the boy's small bucket still where he left it, overflowing.
Real details worth showing: an elevated railway (steel trestle) runs right past the tank; houses, a horse stable, kids, cobbled
Commercial Street, the harbour with a moored ship, snow patches melting on a warm sunny day.
